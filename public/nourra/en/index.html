Inside the facility

An AI data centre does two things: it gets power into the rack and takes heat out.

Switch between the power and cooling paths, and select a component to read about it.

  • Power
  • Coolant supply
  • Coolant return
Power path · 1 / 5

Grid connection

The facility draws high-voltage power from the grid. How much capacity is available, and whether there are two independent feeds, is the first thing to confirm when choosing a site.

Power path · 2 / 5

Transformers

They step high voltage down to what equipment can use. Total transformer capacity is, in practice, the ceiling of the facility.

Power path · 3 / 5

Uninterruptible power

Batteries carry the load the instant the grid drops, until backup generators start. A GPU training run that loses power loses days of computation.

Power path · 4 / 5

Power distribution

Power is split to every row and every rack. High-density racks need heavier busbars and a higher distribution rating.

Where both paths end

Racks and GPU servers

Where the compute is. One current full-rack GPU system draws as much as a dozen or more conventional racks used to.

Cooling path · 2 / 3

Coolant distribution unit

The heart of a liquid-cooled hall. It sends coolant to the cold plates in every server and brings the heated liquid back.

Cooling path · 1 / 3

Heat rejection

Where the heat finally leaves the building. In a tropical climate like Singapore’s, this stage has a direct effect on the power bill.

Supply chain

How fast a facility gets built depends, as much as anything, on when the equipment arrives.

We procure and deliver power distribution, liquid cooling and prefabricated modules as one package.

Step 1 of 5

Specification

Rack density and site conditions set the power architecture, cooling method and module specification. The equipment list is fixed alongside the engineering drawings.

Nourra’s part

We turn site conditions and client requirements into one complete equipment list.

Step 2 of 5

Manufacture and prefabrication

Switchgear, liquid-cooling equipment and data hall modules are built, pre-assembled and factory-tested, which cuts the work left for site.

Nourra’s part

We integrate a global supply chain with local production in Southeast Asia, and manage schedule and quality as one.

Step 3 of 5

Logistics and customs

Cross-border transport, customs clearance and arrival dates for heavy equipment have to line up with construction progress on site.

Nourra’s part

We coordinate shipping and arrival sequence, under applicable trade and export control rules.

Step 4 of 5

Installation and commissioning

Modules are set in place, connected to power and pipework, and commissioned with the rest of the facility.

Nourra’s part

Installed by our own engineering crews and commissioned together with the manufacturer.

Step 5 of 5

Maintenance and spares

Scheduled servicing, repair and spare parts once the equipment is in operation.

Nourra’s part

We set up spares stock and a maintenance plan, carried out by the on-site team.

Delivery

A cluster passes six gates between site selection and go-live.

01/ 06
Site and power
  1. Gate 1

    Site and power

    Confirm land use, floor loading, ceiling height and available power. Until power is secured, nothing after it stands.

    Nourra’s partSite survey, and checks on power capacity, permits and power-on dates.

  2. Gate 2

    Design and approvals

    Fix rack density, the power architecture and the cooling scheme, then complete drawings and approvals.

    Nourra’s partWe organise design and coordinate approvals.

  3. Gate 3

    Building and M&E

    Structural strengthening, transformers and switchgear, uninterruptible power, cooling pipework and fire protection.

    Nourra’s partBuilt by our own engineering crews and accepted at milestones.

  4. Gate 4

    Arrival and racking

    Servers, network equipment and full-rack systems arrive on site to be unpacked, racked, cabled and powered.

    Nourra’s partWe verify supply and arrival dates. Our own crews rack and cable.

  5. Gate 5

    Commissioning

    Network connectivity, full-load running and thermal validation. Problems found here cost far less than problems found after go-live.

    Nourra’s partWe commission alongside specialist engineers and issue test records.

  6. Gate 6

    Handover and operations

    The cluster is handed to its user and enters long-term operation.

    Nourra’s partOur on-site team operates it against a service level agreement.

Principles

Three principles we work by.

  • End to end

    From site selection to maintenance, the critical work is done by our own people. Responsibility stays in one place.

  • Compliance

    All business follows applicable export control and sanctions law. We confirm end user, location and use before we proceed.

  • Long-term partners

    We work with a limited number of partners over the long term, on written terms.
